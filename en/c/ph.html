Philippines events by month (2027)

  1. January

    • Jan 1 Public holiday New Year: New Year's Eve and New Year's Day holidays, with fireworks across Manila.
    • Jan 17 Festival Sinulog Festival, Cebu: A huge street-dance parade on the third Sunday of January.
    • Jan 17 Festival Ati-Atihan Festival, Kalibo: The "mother of Philippine festivals", with street dancing in blackface paint.
    • Jan 24 Festival Dinagyang Festival, Iloilo: Tribal-costume dance competition on the fourth Sunday of January.

  2. February

    • Feb 1–28 Festival Panagbenga Flower Festival, Baguio: Panagbenga flower-float parades.
    • Feb 6 Public holiday Chinese New Year: Special holiday; Manila's Chinatown is the liveliest.

  3. March

    • Mar 9 (approx.) Public holiday Eid al-Fitr: Public holiday.
    • Mar 21–28 Public holiday Holy Week: The most important holiday in this Catholic country; many businesses close.
    • Mar 21–28 (approx.) Festival Moriones Festival, Marinduque: During Holy Week, islanders wear Roman soldier masks and re-enact Bible stories.

  4. April

    • Apr 9 Public holiday Day of Valor: Commemorates the Battle of Bataan.

  5. May

    • May 1 Public holiday Labour Day: Public holiday.
    • May 15 Festival Pahiyas Festival, Lucban: Houses are decorated with colourful rice wafers, fruit and vegetables.
    • May 16 (approx.) Public holiday Eid al-Adha: Public holiday.

  6. June

    • Jun 12 Public holiday Independence Day: Marks the declaration of independence in 1898.
    • Jun 24 Festival San Juan water-splashing festival: San Juan City in Manila's water festival, with people drenching each other in the streets.

  7. August

    • Aug 14–16 (approx.) Festival Kadayawan Festival, Davao: Tribal dances and floats celebrating the fruit and flower harvest.
    • Aug 21 Public holiday Ninoy Aquino Day: Special holiday.
    • Aug 30 Public holiday National Heroes Day: Last Monday of August.

  8. September

    • Sep 17–25 (approx.) Festival Peñafrancia Festival, Naga: The Virgin's feast in Naga, southern Luzon; a fluvial procession draws hundreds of thousands of pilgrims.

  9. October

    • Oct 1–25 (approx.) Festival MassKara Festival, Bacolod: MassKara: street dancing in smiling masks.

  10. November

    • Nov 1–May 31 Season Boracay peak season: Calmer seas in the northeast-monsoon months; the beaches are at their best.
    • Nov 1–2 Public holiday All Saints' & All Souls' Days: Families visit graves nationwide; cemeteries are busy through the night and traffic is heavy.
    • Nov 30 Public holiday Bonifacio Day: Public holiday.

  11. December

    • Dec 8 Public holiday Immaculate Conception: Special holiday.
    • Dec 16–24 Festival Simbang Gabi (dawn Masses): Dawn Masses on the nine days before Christmas, with rice-cake stalls outside the churches.
    • Dec 18 (approx.) Festival Giant Lantern Festival, San Fernando: A giant-lantern contest in San Fernando, Pampanga, with patterns that change to music.
    • Dec 24–25 Public holiday Christmas: The Philippine Christmas season starts in September; Christmas Eve and Christmas Day are national holidays.
    • Dec 30 Public holiday Rizal Day: Honours the national hero José Rizal.
