Thailand events by month (2027)

  1. January

    • Jan 1 Public holiday New Year holidays: New Year holidays; crowds at Bangkok's countdowns and on the islands.
    • Jan 9 Public holiday Children's Day: Second Saturday of January; government offices open to visitors, but it isn't a day off.
    • Jan 15–17 (approx.) Festival Bo Sang Umbrella Festival: Annual festival in Bo Sang's handmade paper-umbrella village near Chiang Mai, with umbrella parades and painting.

  2. February

    • Feb 5–7 Festival Chiang Mai Flower Festival: Flower-float parade on the first weekend of February.
    • Feb 6–7 (approx.) Festival Chinese New Year on Yaowarat Road, Bangkok: Yaowarat Road in Bangkok's Chinatown closes to traffic for lanterns and lion dances.
    • Feb 21–22 Public holiday Makha Bucha: Buddhist holiday with a nationwide alcohol ban and candlelit processions at temples in the evening.

  3. March

    • Mar 13–15 (approx.) Festival Pattaya International Music Festival: Big free concerts on Pattaya Beach.

  4. April

    • Apr 6 Public holiday Chakri Day: Public holiday.
    • Apr 12–16 Festival Songkran on Chiang Mai's moat: The area around Chiang Mai's moat has Thailand's liveliest water fights.
    • Apr 13–15 Public holiday Songkran (water festival): Thai New Year with nationwide water fights; liveliest in Chiang Mai and Bangkok.

  5. May

    • May 1–3 Public holiday Labour Day: A holiday for private companies.
    • May 1–Oct 31 Season Phuket rainy low season: Rainy season on the west coast brings big waves; many island trips stop and rooms are cheap.
    • May 4 Public holiday Coronation Day: Public holiday.
    • May 8–10 (approx.) Festival Rocket Festival (Bun Bang Fai): Home-made rockets are launched to bring the rains in Yasothon, on the second weekend of May.
    • May 20 Public holiday Visakha Bucha (Buddha's Birthday): The most important Buddhist holiday, with a nationwide alcohol ban.

  6. June

    • Jun 3 Public holiday Queen Suthida's Birthday: Public holiday.
    • Jun 4–6 (approx.) Festival Hua Hin Jazz Festival: Jazz festival on the beach at Hua Hin, a seaside town with royal roots.
    • Jun 26–28 (approx.) Festival Phi Ta Khon (ghost festival): Parade of painted ghost masks in Dan Sai, Loei.

  7. July

    • Jul 18–19 (approx.) Festival Ubon Candle Festival: A parade of giant wax sculptures for Buddhist Lent.
    • Jul 18–20 Public holiday Asahna Bucha & Buddhist Lent Day: Two consecutive Buddhist holidays with a nationwide alcohol ban; the Ubon Candle Festival takes place at the same time.
    • Jul 28 Public holiday King's Birthday: Public holiday, with portraits of the King and yellow flags everywhere.

  8. August

    • Aug 12 Public holiday Mother's Day (Queen Mother Sirikit's Birthday): Public holiday.

  9. September

    • Sep 30–Oct 8 (approx.) Festival Phuket Vegetarian Festival: From the 1st to the 9th of the 9th lunar month, devotees parade with face-piercing rituals.

  10. October

    • Oct 13 Public holiday King Bhumibol Memorial Day: Commemorates the passing of the late King; public holiday.
    • Oct 23–25 Public holiday Chulalongkorn Day: Public holiday, with flowers laid at King Chulalongkorn's equestrian statue in Bangkok.

  11. November

    • Nov 1–Feb 28 Season Peak season (dry season): Cool and dry, the best time to travel, and the most expensive.
    • Nov 13–14 (approx.) Festival Loy Krathong & Yi Peng lantern festival, Chiang Mai: Floating krathong offerings; Chiang Mai holds the Yi Peng lantern festival at the same time.
    • Nov 14–15 (approx.) Festival Surin Elephant Round-up: Hundreds of elephants perform and parade on the third weekend of November.

  12. December

    • Dec 1–31 (approx.) Festival Bangkok Christmas & New Year lights: Big light displays around Siam and ICONSIAM, with fireworks on the Chao Phraya at New Year.
    • Dec 5–6 Public holiday Father's Day (National Day): The late King Bhumibol's birthday; public holiday.
    • Dec 10 Public holiday Constitution Day: Public holiday.
