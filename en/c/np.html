Nepal events by month (2027)

  1. January

    • Jan 11 Public holiday Prithvi Jayanti: Honours the king who unified Nepal.
    • Jan 15 Public holiday Maghe Sankranti: A post-solstice festival with ritual bathing at rivers.
    • Jan 29 Public holiday Martyrs' Day: Public holiday.

  2. February

    • Feb 7 Public holiday Lhosar (Sonam & Gyalpo Lhosar): Liveliest around Boudhanath Stupa.
    • Feb 19 Public holiday Democracy Day: Public holiday.

  3. March

    • Mar 6 Public holiday Maha Shivaratri: Crowds of sadhus and devotees at Pashupatinath Temple; a national holiday.
    • Mar 8 Public holiday Women's Day: A holiday for women.
    • Mar 10 (approx.) Public holiday Eid al-Fitr: Public holiday.
    • Mar 21–22 Public holiday Holi (Fagu Purnima): The festival of coloured powder; liveliest in Kathmandu Durbar Square.

  4. April

    • Apr 10–18 (approx.) Festival Bisket Jatra, Bhaktapur: Bhaktapur's big festival around Nepali New Year, with chariot-pulling and a giant pole raised.
    • Apr 14 Public holiday Nepali New Year: Bikram Sambat New Year; Bhaktapur holds Bisket Jatra.
    • Apr 15 Public holiday Ram Navami: Hindu festival.

  5. May

    • May 1 Public holiday Labour Day: Public holiday.
    • May 17 (approx.) Public holiday Eid al-Adha: Public holiday.
    • May 20 Public holiday Buddha Purnima: Liveliest at Lumbini and Swayambhunath.
    • May 20 (approx.) Festival Buddha Jayanti at Lumbini: Pilgrimages and ceremonies at Lumbini, the Buddha's birthplace.
    • May 29 Public holiday Republic Day: Public holiday.

  6. August

    • Aug 17 Public holiday Janai Purnima: Hindu festival when devotees change their sacred thread.
    • Aug 25 Public holiday Janmashtami: Liveliest at the Krishna Mandir in Patan Durbar Square.

  7. September

    • Sep 15–22 (approx.) Festival Indra Jatra: The living goddess Kumari's chariot is pulled through Kathmandu Durbar Square.
    • Sep 19 Public holiday Constitution Day: Public holiday.
    • Sep 30–Oct 14 (approx.) Festival Dashain: Nepal's biggest festival, lasting 15 days.

  8. October

    • Oct 1–Nov 30 Season Trekking peak season: Clear skies after the monsoon give the best Himalayan views.
    • Oct 6–11 Public holiday Dashain public holidays: The main days of Dashain are national holidays; many shops close and long-distance bus tickets are hard to get.
    • Oct 27–31 (approx.) Festival Tihar (festival of lights): Five days honouring crows, dogs and cows, with every house lit up.
    • Oct 29–31 Public holiday Tihar public holidays: The later days of Tihar are national holidays, and every home is lit with lamps.

  9. November

    • Nov 4 Public holiday Chhath Parva: The biggest festival of the southern plains, with sun worship at riverbanks.
    • Nov 10–12 (approx.) Festival Mani Rimdu: Masked dances at Tengboche Monastery in the Everest region, in trekking season.

  10. December

    • Dec 25 Public holiday Christmas: Public holiday.
    • Dec 28–Jan 1 (approx.) Festival Pokhara Street Festival: Pokhara's Lakeside becomes a car-free food and performance street, liveliest at New Year.
    • Dec 30 Public holiday Tamu Lhosar: Gurung New Year, with parades in Pokhara.
