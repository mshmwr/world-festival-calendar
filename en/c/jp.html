Japan events by month (2027)

  1. January

    • Jan 1–3 Public holiday New Year & Hatsumode: Japan's most important holiday: shrine visits for the new year, and lucky bags in department stores.
    • Jan 1–Feb 28 Season Zao snow monsters (Yamagata): Snow monsters (frost-covered trees), lit up at night.
    • Jan 1–2 Festival Takasaki Daruma Market (Gunma): At Shorinzan Daruma-ji, home of Japan's daruma dolls.
    • Jan 2 Festival New Year Greeting at the Imperial Palace (Tokyo): The Emperor and Imperial family greet the public from the Chowaden balcony.
    • Jan 2–3 Festival Hakone Ekiden (Kanagawa): A university relay between Tokyo and Hakone, ten runners per team; outbound from Otemachi to Lake Ashi on the 2nd, back on the 3rd. The mountain 5th leg is the highlight.
    • Jan 9–11 Festival Toka Ebisu, Imamiya (Osaka): A million worshippers come to pray for business success.
    • Jan 10–24 (approx.) Sports January Grand Sumo Tournament (Hatsu Basho) (Tokyo): At the Ryogoku Kokugikan in Tokyo for 15 days; tickets go on sale about a month ahead and popular days sell out fast, or come early to watch wrestlers arrive.
    • Jan 10 Festival Fukuotoko race, Nishinomiya Shrine (Hyogo): When the gates open at dawn, runners sprint 230 m to be named "lucky man".
    • Jan 11 Public holiday Coming of Age Day: Second Monday of January; new adults attend ceremonies in furisode kimono.
    • Jan 15 Festival Nozawa Onsen Dosojin Fire Festival (Nagano): One of Japan's three great fire festivals, a battle of attack and defence around the shrine.
    • Jan 17–Feb 8 (approx.) Festival Shirakawa-go light-up (Gifu): Lit up on selected winter weekend nights; viewpoint and bus reservations required.
    • Jan 20–Mar 10 Season Okhotsk drift ice season (Hokkaido): Board icebreakers from Abashiri or Monbetsu to see the drift ice, best in February.
    • Jan 20–Feb 10 (approx.) Festival Motobu Yaedake Cherry Blossom Festival (Okinawa): Japan's earliest cherry blossoms (Taiwan cherry).
    • Jan 23 Festival Wakakusa Yamayaki (Nara): On the fourth Saturday of January the whole hillside is set on fire.
    • Jan 24–Feb 3 (approx.) Festival Jozankei Snow Lantern Path (Hokkaido): At an onsen town near Sapporo, snow lanterns fill Jozan Gensen Park and the riverbanks.
    • Jan 27–Feb 20 (approx.) Festival Nagasaki Lantern Festival (Nagasaki): 15,000 Chinese lanterns over Lunar New Year.
    • Jan 30–Feb 22 (approx.) Festival Kintetsu "Tsudoi" footbath train (Mie): Sightseeing train from Kintetsu Nagoya to Yunoyama Onsen with cypress-wood footbaths on board, filled with Komono hot-spring water. Runs only on weekends and holidays in January and February; 2027 dates are yet to be announced by Kintetsu.

  2. February

    • Feb 1–Mar 10 Season Dazaifu plum blossoms (Fukuoka): 6,000 plum trees at the Tenmangu, including the "flying plum".
    • Feb 3 Festival Setsubun: Beans are thrown to drive out demons ("Oni wa soto, fuku wa uchi"), and people eat ehomaki rolls.
    • Feb 3 Festival Setsubun Mantoro, Kasuga Taisha (Nara): 3,000 lanterns lit at once.
    • Feb 4–11 (approx.) Festival Sapporo Snow Festival (Hokkaido): Giant snow sculptures in Odori Park and ice sculptures in Susukino: Hokkaido's signature winter event.
    • Feb 8–15 (approx.) Festival Otaru Snow Light Path (Hokkaido): Snow lanterns line the Otaru Canal and the old Temiya railway line.
    • Feb 9–12 (approx.) Festival Hirosaki Castle Snow Lantern Festival (Aomori): Snow lanterns and lit snow sculptures inside Hirosaki Castle.
    • Feb 10–Mar 10 (approx.) Festival Kawazu Cherry Blossom Festival (Shizuoka): Early pink Kawazu cherry blossoms and rapeseed flowers.
    • Feb 11 Public holiday National Foundation Day: Public holiday.
    • Feb 11–Mar 20 (approx.) Festival Mito Plum Festival (Ibaraki): About 3,000 plum trees at Kairakuen and Kodokan.
    • Feb 12–14 Festival Namahage Sedo Festival, Oga (Akita): Namahage descend the mountain with torches from Shinzan Shrine.
    • Feb 13–15 (approx.) Festival Tokamachi Snow Festival (Niigata): The birthplace of Japan's modern snow festivals, with shows on snow stages.
    • Feb 15–16 Festival Yokote Kamakura Festival (Akita): Hundreds of kamakura snow huts and tiny candlelit snow lanterns.
    • Feb 19 (approx.) Festival Konomiya Naked Festival (Aichi): On the 13th day of the first lunar month, ten thousand nearly naked men try to touch the "shin-otoko".
    • Feb 20 Festival Saidaiji Eyo (naked festival) (Okayama): One of Japan's three great unusual festivals: ten thousand nearly naked men scramble for sacred sticks.
    • Feb 23 Public holiday Emperor's Birthday: The Emperor greets the public at the Imperial Palace.

  3. March

    • Mar 1–14 Festival Shuni-e (Omizutori), Todai-ji (Nara): Unbroken for 1,200 years, with giant pine torches at night.
    • Mar 1–15 (approx.) Festival Kurashiki Shunsho Akari (Okayama): The Bikan Quarter lit with bamboo lanterns.
    • Mar 1–20 Season Aso grassland burning (Kumamoto): A thousand-year tradition of burning the grasslands to keep them open.
    • Mar 3 Festival Hinamatsuri (Doll Festival): Hina dolls are displayed to wish girls good health.
    • Mar 7 Sports Tokyo Marathon: One of the World Marathon Majors; ballot entries open around August. In 2027 it also hosts the Age Group World Championships.
    • Mar 14–28 (approx.) Sports March Grand Sumo Tournament (Haru Basho) (Osaka): At Osaka Prefectural Gymnasium (Edion Arena) for 15 days; tickets go on sale about a month ahead and popular days sell out fast, or come early to watch wrestlers arrive.
    • Mar 15–May 31 Season Best season for the Shikoku pilgrimage (Tokushima): The 88-temple pilgrimage begins at Ryozen-ji in Tokushima.
    • Mar 19–21 (approx.) Festival Dogo Onsen Festival (Ehime): Hot-spring thanksgiving festival with portable shrines.
    • Mar 20–Apr 15 Season Cherry blossom season: The bloom moves north from Kyushu; Tokyo and Kyoto peak around late March to early April.
    • Mar 20–May 31 Season First bonito season (Kochi): The best season for straw-seared bonito.
    • Mar 20–Oct 31 Season Okinawa beach season (Okinawa): Beaches open for swimming after the official season opening.
    • Mar 21 Public holiday Vernal Equinox Day: Spring equinox week, a time for visiting family graves.

  4. April

    • Apr 1–20 Season Yoshino cherry blossoms (Nara): 30,000 cherry trees bloom up the slopes from Shimo-senbon to Oku-senbon.
    • Apr 1–5 (approx.) Festival Beppu Hatto Onsen Festival (Oita): Free hot springs and the Ogiyama fire festival.
    • Apr 3–5 (approx.) Festival Shingen-ko Festival (Yamanashi): A procession of Takeda Shingen's army in Kofu.
    • Apr 4–5 (approx.) Festival Inuyama Festival (Aichi): 13 floats parade below Inuyama Castle.
    • Apr 5–20 Season Takato cherry blossoms (Nagano): "The best cherry blossoms under heaven": Kohigan cherries.
    • Apr 9–11 Sports F1 Japanese Grand Prix (Suzuka): Formula 1 at Suzuka Circuit in Mie, often during cherry blossom season; book Kintetsu trains from Nagoya and hotels early.
    • Apr 10–25 Season Miharu Takizakura (Fukushima): A cherry tree over a thousand years old, one of Japan's three great cherry trees.
    • Apr 10–May 5 (approx.) Festival Chichibu Shibazakura Festival (Saitama): 400,000 moss phlox plants at Hitsujiyama Park.
    • Apr 11–May 31 (approx.) Festival Fuji Shibazakura Festival (Yamanashi): 500,000 moss phlox plants by Lake Motosu, with Mt Fuji behind.
    • Apr 12–19 (approx.) Festival Kamakura Festival (Kanagawa): Horseback archery and the Shizuka dance at Tsurugaoka Hachimangu.
    • Apr 13–16 Festival Nagahama Hikiyama Festival (Shiga): Children's kabuki performed on the floats; on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.
    • Apr 14–15 Festival Takayama Spring Festival (Gifu): One of Japan's three most beautiful festivals, with ornate floats and mechanical puppets.
    • Apr 15–May 10 Season Nemophila at Hitachi Seaside Park (Ibaraki): Whole hillsides covered in blue nemophila.
    • Apr 15–May 15 Season Wisteria at Ashikaga Flower Park (Tochigi): A 160-year-old giant wisteria, lit up at night.
    • Apr 15–Jun 25 Season Tateyama Kurobe snow corridor (Toyama): A walkway between snow walls up to 20 m high.
    • Apr 18–May 5 (approx.) Festival Hirosaki Cherry Blossom Festival (Aomori): About 2,600 cherry trees at Hirosaki Castle; petals carpeting the moat are a famous sight.
    • Apr 20–May 10 Season Kawachi Fujien wisteria (Fukuoka): A wisteria tunnel; reservations required.
    • Apr 22–May 5 (approx.) Festival Tonami Tulip Fair (Toyama): Three million tulips.
    • Apr 25–26 (approx.) Festival Tendo Cherry Blossom Festival human shogi (Yamagata): Shogi played with people in samurai costume as the pieces.
    • Apr 27 Festival Kamikochi opening ceremony (Nagano): A ceremony at Kappa Bridge praying for safe climbing.
    • Apr 29–May 5 Public holiday Golden Week: Peak domestic travel; flights and hotels cost more, so it's best avoided.
    • Apr 29 Public holiday Showa Day: The first day of Golden Week.
    • Apr 29 Festival Kintaikyo Festival (Yamaguchi): A recreated daimyo procession crosses the bridge.
    • Apr 29–May 5 Festival Arita Ceramic Fair (Saga): A huge ceramics fair with hundreds of kilns.

  5. May

    • May 1–5 Festival Spring Fujiwara Festival (Iwate): A re-enactment of Minamoto no Yoshitsune's arrival in Hiraizumi.
    • May 2–4 Festival Sentei Festival, Shimonoseki (Yamaguchi): Oiran processions and the Joro Sanpai.
    • May 3 Public holiday Constitution Memorial Day: Golden Week holidays.
    • May 3–5 Festival Hamamatsu Festival (Shizuoka): Giant kite battles on the Nakatajima sand dunes.
    • May 3–5 Festival Hiroshima Flower Festival (Hiroshima): Float parade along Peace Boulevard.
    • May 3–4 Festival Hakata Dontaku (Fukuoka): The festival that draws the most people during Golden Week.
    • May 4 Public holiday Greenery Day: Golden Week holidays.
    • May 5 Public holiday Children's Day: Carp streamers fly to wish boys good health.
    • May 9–23 (approx.) Sports May Grand Sumo Tournament (Natsu Basho) (Tokyo): At the Ryogoku Kokugikan in Tokyo for 15 days; tickets go on sale about a month ahead and popular days sell out fast, or come early to watch wrestlers arrive.
    • May 11–Oct 15 Season Nagara River cormorant fishing (Gifu): A thousand-year tradition of cormorant fishing, by masters attached to the Imperial Household Agency.
    • May 13–16 (approx.) Festival Kanda Matsuri (Tokyo): One of Edo's three great festivals; the main festival is held in odd years, with portable shrines passing through Akihabara.
    • May 14–16 (approx.) Festival Sanja Matsuri, Asakusa (Tokyo): A hundred portable shrines circle Asakusa on the third weekend of May.
    • May 15–16 (approx.) Festival Sendai Aoba Festival (Miyagi): Float parades and sparrow dances honouring Date Masamune.
    • May 15–17 (approx.) Festival Shimoda Black Ship Festival (Shizuoka): Marks the arrival of Perry's fleet, with Japan–US exchange events.
    • May 15 Festival Aoi Matsuri, Kyoto (Kyoto): One of Kyoto's three great festivals: a procession in Heian-era dress from the Imperial Palace to Kamigamo Shrine.
    • May 17–18 Festival Nikko Toshogu Spring Grand Festival (Tochigi): A thousand-warrior procession and horseback archery.
    • May 19–21 Festival Mikuni Festival (Fukui): One of Hokuriku's three great festivals, with giant warrior-doll floats.
    • May 20–Jun 20 Season Skunk cabbage at Oze (Gunma): Marshes filled with white skunk cabbage.
    • May 23–25 (approx.) Festival Soma Nomaoi (Fukushima): Hundreds of riders in armour race and compete for flags; held in May since 2024.

  6. June

    • Jun 1–30 Season Kamakura hydrangeas (Kanagawa): Hydrangea season at Meigetsu-in and Hase-dera.
    • Jun 1–Sep 20 Season Ozu cormorant fishing (Ehime): Watch cormorant fishing from roofed boats on the Hiji River.
    • Jun 5 Festival Kanazawa Hyakumangoku Festival (Ishikawa): A procession recreating Maeda Toshiie's entry into the castle.
    • Jun 7–17 (2026) (approx.) Festival Sanno Matsuri (Tokyo): Hie Shrine's festival, with the grand procession in even years.
    • Jun 10–14 (approx.) Festival Yosakoi Soran Festival (Hokkaido): A naruko dance contest in Sapporo's Odori Park with tens of thousands of dancers.
    • Jun 12 Festival Chagu Chagu Umakko (Iwate): About a hundred decorated horses walk from Takizawa to Morioka Hachimangu.

  7. July

    • Jul 1–31 Season Furano lavender season (Hokkaido): Rainbow flower fields at Farm Tomita and Shikisai-no-oka, best in mid-July.
    • Jul 1–Oct 15 Season Noto Kiriko lantern festivals (Ishikawa): Over 200 giant-lantern festivals across Noto in summer.
    • Jul 1–31 Festival Gion Matsuri, Kyoto (Kyoto): One of Japan's three great festivals; the float parades on 17 and 24 July are the highlight.
    • Jul 1–15 Festival Hakata Gion Yamakasa (Fukuoka): At dawn on the 15th comes the Oiyama race, with men running a one-tonne float.
    • Jul 3–5 (approx.) Festival Shonan Hiratsuka Tanabata Festival (Kanagawa): The largest Tanabata festival in the Kanto region.
    • Jul 7 Festival Tanabata: Wishes are written on strips of paper and hung on bamboo; Sendai and some other places celebrate in August.
    • Jul 10–12 (approx.) Festival Narita Gion Festival (Chiba): Floats climb the Omotesando approach to Naritasan.
    • Jul 10–12 (approx.) Festival Sawara Grand Festival (summer) (Chiba): Floats topped with giant figures, on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.
    • Jul 10–Sep 10 Season Mt Fuji climbing season (Yamanashi): The Yoshida Trail on the Yamanashi side opens; reservations and a climbing fee required.
    • Jul 11–Sep 5 (approx.) Festival Gujo Odori (Gifu): Over thirty nights of bon dancing, all night long from 13 to 16 August.
    • Jul 11–25 (approx.) Sports July Grand Sumo Tournament (Nagoya Basho) (Aichi): At IG Arena (Aichi International Arena) in Nagoya for 15 days; tickets go on sale about a month ahead and popular days sell out fast, or come early to watch wrestlers arrive.
    • Jul 14 Festival Nachi Fire Festival (Wakayama): Twelve huge torches purify the approach before Nachi Falls.
    • Jul 19 Public holiday Marine Day: Third Monday of July, the start of beach season.
    • Jul 20 (approx.) Festival Kangensai, Miyajima (Hiroshima): On the 17th day of the 6th lunar month, the sacred boat plays court music on the sea.
    • Jul 20–27 Festival Heron Dance, Yamaguchi Gion Festival (Yamaguchi): The heron dance at Yasaka Shrine.
    • Jul 20–Aug 7 (approx.) Festival Kirishima International Music Festival (Kagoshima): A classical music festival in the Kirishima mountains.
    • Jul 24–Nov 7 (approx.) Festival Echigo-Tsumari Art Field (Daichi no Geijutsusai) (Niigata): Echigo-Tsumari Art Triennale: large outdoor artworks among farms and rice terraces.
    • Jul 24–25 Festival Tenjin Matsuri, Osaka (Osaka): One of Japan's three great festivals; on the 25th, a boat procession and fireworks after dark.
    • Jul 25–26 (approx.) Festival Owari Tsushima Tenno Festival (Aichi): Boats bearing semicircular domes of lanterns float at night.
    • Jul 31–Aug 4 Festival Hachinohe Sansha Taisai (Aomori): A parade of 27 ornate floats, on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.
    • Jul 31 (approx.) Festival Sumida River Fireworks (Tokyo): Tokyo's most famous fireworks, on the last Saturday of July.

  8. August

    • Aug 1–7 Festival Hirosaki Neputa Festival (Aomori): Fan-shaped warrior lanterns, in a different style from Aomori's Nebuta.
    • Aug 1–4 Festival Morioka Sansa Odori (Iwate): The world's largest taiko drum parade, per Guinness World Records.
    • Aug 1–2 (approx.) Festival Okayama Momotaro Festival (Okayama): Ura dance and fireworks.
    • Aug 2–7 Festival Aomori Nebuta Festival (Aomori): A parade of giant illuminated warrior floats, one of Tohoku's three great festivals; rent a yukata and dance along.
    • Aug 2–3 Festival Nagaoka Festival Grand Fireworks (Niigata): One of Japan's three great fireworks shows, including the memorial "Phoenix" fireworks.
    • Aug 3–6 Festival Akita Kanto Festival (Akita): Tall poles hung with lanterns balanced on palms, foreheads and shoulders; one of Tohoku's three great festivals.
    • Aug 3–5 Festival Numata Festival (Gunma): A tengu portable shrine carried by women.
    • Aug 5–7 Festival Yamagata Hanagasa Festival (Yamagata): Ten thousand dancers with flower hats dance through the streets.
    • Aug 5–14 (approx.) Festival Nara Tokae (Nara): 20,000 candles in Nara Park.
    • Aug 6–8 Festival Sendai Tanabata Festival (Miyagi): Shopping arcades hung with huge washi Tanabata streamers; one of Tohoku's three great festivals.
    • Aug 6 Festival Hiroshima Peace Memorial Ceremony (Hiroshima): Atomic Bomb Memorial Day; lanterns are floated on the Motoyasu River in the evening.
    • Aug 7–8 (approx.) Festival Fukushima Waraji Festival (Fukushima): A parade carrying a 12 m giant straw sandal.
    • Aug 7–9 (approx.) Festival Niigata Festival (Niigata): Folk-dance parade over Bandai Bridge and fireworks.
    • Aug 7 (approx.) Festival Lake Biwa Fireworks (Shiga): Fireworks over the lake from Otsu Port.
    • Aug 7–9 (approx.) Festival Matsuyama Festival (Ehime): A parade of Yakyuken dancers.
    • Aug 9–12 Festival Yosakoi Festival, Kochi (Kochi): A dance contest with naruko clappers, in the birthplace of Yosakoi.
    • Aug 9 Festival Nagasaki Peace Ceremony (Nagasaki): Atomic Bomb Memorial Day.
    • Aug 11 Public holiday Mountain Day: Public holiday leading into the Obon holidays.
    • Aug 12–15 Festival Awa Odori, Tokushima (Tokushima): Japan's biggest bon dance: "Fools who dance and fools who watch".
    • Aug 12–14 Festival Takamatsu Festival (Kagawa): Mass dancing and fireworks.
    • Aug 13–16 Public holiday Obon: Families return home to honour ancestors; bon dances and farewell fires everywhere, with Kyoto's Gozan Okuribi on 8/16.
    • Aug 13 Festival Mandoku-yo lantern rite, Koyasan (Wakayama): 100,000 candles along the approach to Okunoin.
    • Aug 13 Festival Kanmon Strait Fireworks (Yamaguchi): Fireworks launched from both Shimonoseki and Moji at once.
    • Aug 14–15 Festival Chugen Mantoro, Kasuga Taisha (Nara): The summer lantern festival.
    • Aug 14–15 (approx.) Festival Tottori Shan-Shan Umbrella Festival (Tottori): Dancing with umbrellas hung with bells.
    • Aug 15 Festival Lake Suwa Fireworks Festival (Nagano): Big fireworks reflected on the lake.
    • Aug 15 Festival Shoro Nagashi, Nagasaki (Nagasaki): Spirit boats are sent off amid firecrackers; very lively.
    • Aug 15–16 Festival Yamaga Lantern Festival (Kumamoto): A thousand women dance with golden lanterns on their heads.
    • Aug 16 Festival Gozan no Okuribi (Kyoto): Bonfires in shapes such as "大" are lit on five mountains to send off ancestral spirits.
    • Aug 17 Festival Kumano Fireworks Festival (Mie): Fireworks between the sea and the cliffs of Shichiri-mihama.
    • Aug 26–27 Festival Yoshida Fire Festival (Yamanashi): The festival closing Mt Fuji's season, with 3 m torches lighting the streets.
    • Aug 28 Festival Omagari National Fireworks Competition (Akita): One of Japan's three great fireworks shows, a national competition between pyrotechnicians.
    • Aug 28–29 (approx.) Festival Koenji Awa Odori (Tokyo): Tokyo's biggest Awa Odori.

  9. September

    • Sep 1–3 Festival Owara Kaze no Bon, Yatsuo (Toyama): Silent dancing to the kokyu fiddle, most atmospheric at night.
    • Sep 10–30 Season Daisetsuzan autumn leaves (Hokkaido): Japan's earliest autumn leaves; Mt Asahi and Mt Kurodake peak in mid-September.
    • Sep 11–13 (approx.) Festival Okinawa Zento Eisa Festival (Okinawa): A taiko dance festival in Okinawa City, held after Obon.
    • Sep 12–13 (approx.) Festival Jozenji Streetjazz Festival (Miyagi): Hundreds of bands play for free on the streets of Sendai.
    • Sep 12–26 (approx.) Sports September Grand Sumo Tournament (Aki Basho) (Tokyo): At the Ryogoku Kokugikan in Tokyo for 15 days; tickets go on sale about a month ahead and popular days sell out fast, or come early to watch wrestlers arrive.
    • Sep 12–18 Festival Hojoya at Hakozaki-gu (Fukuoka): Kyushu's biggest autumn festival, with hundreds of stalls along the approach.
    • Sep 14–20 (approx.) Festival Fujisaki Hachimangu Autumn Festival (Kumamoto): Kumamoto's biggest autumn festival, with warrior processions and decorated horses.
    • Sep 18–19 Festival Kishiwada Danjiri Festival (Osaka): Four-tonne danjiri floats are swung round corners at full speed.
    • Sep 19–Oct 4 (2026) Sports Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games: The 20th Asian Games, centred on Nagoya; Japan's third time hosting. The next edition is in Doha, Qatar, in 2030.
    • Sep 20 Public holiday Respect for the Aged Day: Third Monday of September.
    • Sep 20 (approx.) Festival Japan's biggest imoni party (Yamagata): Taro soup cooked in a 6.5 m pot stirred with an excavator.
    • Sep 20–22 (approx.) Festival Aizu Festival (Fukushima): A procession of past lords and the Byakkotai.
    • Sep 22 (2026) Public holiday Citizens' Holiday (Silver Week): Falls between Respect for the Aged Day and the Autumnal Equinox, making a five-day weekend in 2026.
    • Sep 23 Public holiday Autumnal Equinox Day: Autumn equinox week, a time for visiting family graves.
    • Sep 24–25 Festival Gokayama Mugiya Festival (Toyama): Traditional folk dances in the gassho village.

  10. October

    • Oct 1–25 Season Jozankei autumn leaves (Hokkaido): About an hour from Sapporo; Futami suspension bridge and Hoheikyo peak in mid-October; pair with an onsen.
    • Oct 1–25 Season Kochia at Hitachi Seaside Park (Ibaraki): Rounded kochia bushes turn fiery red.
    • Oct 7–9 Festival Nagasaki Kunchi (Nagasaki): Dedicatory dances blending Chinese and Dutch influences; the dragon dance is the favourite.
    • Oct 9–11 (approx.) Festival Sawara Grand Festival (autumn) (Chiba): Autumn float parade.
    • Oct 9–10 Festival Takayama Autumn Festival (Gifu): Hachiman Festival, with floats lit up at night.
    • Oct 9–11 Festival Kotohiragu Grand Festival (Kagawa): The portable shrines come down the steps at night (Osagari).
    • Oct 10 (approx.) Festival Naha Great Tug-of-War (Okinawa): The world's largest straw rope, per Guinness; anyone can join the pull.
    • Oct 11 Public holiday Sports Day: Second Monday of October.
    • Oct 14–15 Festival Nada Fighting Festival (Hyogo): At Matsubara Hachiman Shrine in Himeji, portable shrines are rammed into each other.
    • Oct 15–Nov 5 Season Oirase & Towada autumn leaves (Aomori): Autumn colours along the stream and the lake.
    • Oct 15–17 Festival Kannamesai at Ise Grand Shrine (Mie): The most important rite at Ise Grand Shrine, offering the new harvest.
    • Oct 16–17 Festival Nikko Toshogu Autumn Festival (Tochigi): The autumn thousand-warrior procession.
    • Oct 16–17 Festival Kawagoe Festival (Saitama): Ornate floats face off in the warehouse district.
    • Oct 16–18 Festival Niihama Taiko Festival (Ehime): Three-tonne drum floats crash into each other.
    • Oct 17–18 (approx.) Festival Nagoya Festival (Aichi): A procession of the three unifiers: Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu.
    • Oct 17–May 31 (approx.) Season Nabana no Sato winter illuminations (Mie): Japan's largest tunnel of lights.
    • Oct 18–24 (2026) Sports Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games: The Asian Para Games, held after the Asian Games.
    • Oct 20–Nov 30 Season Autumn leaves season: Starts in Hokkaido in early October; Kyoto is most colourful in mid-to-late November.
    • Oct 22 Festival Kurama Fire Festival (Kyoto): One of Kyoto's three great unusual festivals, with giant torches paraded through the village.
    • Oct 22 Festival Jidai Matsuri, Kyoto (Kyoto): A parade of costumes from a thousand years of Kyoto history.
    • Oct 24–25 (approx.) Festival Miyazaki Jingu Grand Festival (Miyazaki): The divine procession of Emperor Jimmu.
    • Oct 30–Nov 3 (approx.) Festival Saga International Balloon Fiesta (Saga): Asia's largest balloon fiesta.
    • Oct 31–Nov 3 (approx.) Festival Shurijo Festival (Okinawa): A recreation of the Ryukyu royal court procession.

  11. November

    • Nov 1–Mar 15 Season Yukitsuri at Kenroku-en (Ishikawa): Conical snow supports protect the pines, lit up in winter.
    • Nov 1–23 (approx.) Festival Kawaguchiko Autumn Leaves Festival (Yamanashi): The Momiji Corridor lit up.
    • Nov 1–25 Season Yabakei autumn leaves (Oita): Autumn colours at Hitome Hakkei, "eight views at a glance".
    • Nov 2–4 Festival Karatsu Kunchi (Saga): Fourteen lacquered floats, on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.
    • Nov 2–3 Festival Ohara Festival (Kagoshima): Southern Kyushu's biggest festival, with ten thousand people dancing the Ohara-bushi.
    • Nov 3 Public holiday Culture Day: Culture festivals are held across Japan.
    • Nov 3 Festival Hakone Daimyo Procession (Kanagawa): A recreation of an Edo-period daimyo procession through Hakone-Yumoto.
    • Nov 6 (approx.) Festival Tsuchiura National Fireworks Competition (Ibaraki): One of Japan's three great fireworks competitions.
    • Nov 6–Mar 20 Season Snow crab season (Tottori): The snow crab season opens at Sakaiminato and Tottori ports.
    • Nov 7–8 (approx.) Festival Utsunomiya Gyoza Festival (Tochigi): Try gyoza from every shop in one go.
    • Nov 7–Dec 31 (approx.) Festival Osaka Festival of the Lights (Osaka): Lights along Midosuji and a light show in Nakanoshima.
    • Nov 7–8 (approx.) Festival Usuki Takeyoi bamboo lanterns (Oita): 20,000 bamboo lanterns light the castle town.
    • Nov 8–15 (approx.) Festival Kamiari Festival, Izumo Taisha (Shimane): In the 10th lunar month, all the gods of Japan gather at Izumo.
    • Nov 10–Dec 5 Season Kyoto autumn leaves (Kyoto): Tofuku-ji, Eikando and Arashiyama are at their reddest in late November.
    • Nov 14–28 (approx.) Sports November Grand Sumo Tournament (Kyushu Basho) (Fukuoka): At Fukuoka Kokusai Center for 15 days; tickets go on sale about a month ahead and popular days sell out fast, or come early to watch wrestlers arrive.
    • Nov 15 Festival Shichi-Go-San: Children aged three, five and seven visit shrines in kimono.
    • Nov 15–Feb 10 Season Takachiho Yokagura (Miyazaki): Villages perform 33 kagura dances all night; Takachiho Shrine has kagura for visitors every evening.
    • Nov 23 Public holiday Labour Thanksgiving Day: Public holiday.

  12. December

    • Dec 2–3 Festival Chichibu Night Festival (Saitama): One of Japan's three great float festivals, with winter fireworks and floats; on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.
    • Dec 4–31 (approx.) Festival Sendai Pageant of Starlight (Miyagi): Hundreds of thousands of lights in the zelkova trees of Jozenji-dori.
    • Dec 5–14 (approx.) Festival Kobe Luminarie (Hyogo): A corridor of light art commemorating the Great Hanshin earthquake.
    • Dec 12–21 (approx.) Festival Arashiyama Hanatoro (Kyoto): The bamboo grove and Togetsukyo Bridge lit up.
    • Dec 15–Jan 31 Season Echizen daffodil season (Fukui): Daffodils bloom on the cliffs of the Echizen coast.
    • Dec 31 Festival Namahage (New Year's Eve) (Akita): Namahage go door to door asking for lazy children; on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.
