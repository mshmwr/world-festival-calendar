Taiwan events by month (2027)

  1. January

    • Jan 1 Public holiday Founding Day of the Republic of China (New Year's Day): New Year long weekend; big crowds after the Taipei 101 fireworks.

  2. February

    • Feb 4–10 Public holiday Lunar New Year: Family reunions; temples are packed on New Year's morning and festive markets fill the streets.
    • Feb 20 Festival Lantern Festival: Pingxi sky lanterns & Yanshui beehive fireworks: Sky lanterns in Pingxi, beehive fireworks in Yanshui and Handan bombing in Taitung.
    • Feb 20–Mar 7 (approx.) Festival Taiwan Lantern Festival: Taiwan's national lantern festival from Lantern Festival day, held in a different city each year, with a projection show on the main lantern at night.
    • Feb 28–Mar 1 Public holiday Peace Memorial Day: The 2/28 long weekend; domestic trips and flights abroad cost more.

  3. April

    • Apr 4–6 Public holiday Children's Day & Tomb-Sweeping long weekend: A roughly four-day weekend; peak season for flights and domestic transport.
    • Apr 7–16 (approx.) Festival Dajia Mazu Pilgrimage: A nine-day, eight-night walking pilgrimage, Taiwan's largest religious event; dates are set by divination blocks.
    • Apr 15–May 31 (approx.) Festival Hakka Tung Blossom Festival: Tung blossoms cover the hills of Miaoli, Hsinchu and Taichung; best in late April and early May.
    • Apr 30–May 1 Public holiday Labour Day: A day off for workers; from 2026 civil servants also get the day off.

  4. June

    • Jun 7–9 (approx.) Festival Lukang Dragon Boat Festival & races: Dragon-boat races around the festival; Lukang holds its traditional Dragon King rites and performances.
    • Jun 9 Public holiday Dragon Boat Festival: Dragon-boat races around the island; Lukang and Erlong Village in Yilan are the most distinctive.

  5. July

    • Jul 1–Aug 31 (approx.) Festival Taiwan International Balloon Festival, Taitung: Hot-air balloons rise over Luye Highland every morning, with light-show concerts at night.
    • Jul 3–Aug 15 (approx.) Festival Yilan International Children's Folklore & Folkgame Festival: Summer water play and international folk performances at Dongshan River Water Park.

  6. August

    • Aug 2–31 (approx.) Festival Keelung Ghost Festival: Taiwan's best-known Ghost Festival; water lanterns are released at Badouzi on the 14th night of the 7th lunar month.

  7. September

    • Sep 15 Public holiday Mid-Autumn Festival: Barbecues and moon-viewing all over Taiwan.
    • Sep 19 (approx.) Sports Sun Moon Lake International Swimming Carnival: A mass swim across Sun Moon Lake around Mid-Autumn, with tens of thousands taking part.
    • Sep 28 Public holiday Teachers' Day: Restored as a public holiday from 2025.

  8. October

    • Oct 1–Nov 30 (approx.) Festival Sun Moon Lake Fireworks Music Festival: Weekend fireworks with concerts on the shore of Sun Moon Lake.
    • Oct 10–11 Public holiday National Day: National Day long weekend, with a ceremony at the Presidential Office and fireworks in the evening.
    • Oct 25 Public holiday Taiwan Retrocession & Guningtou Victory Memorial Day: Restored as a public holiday from 2025.

  9. November

    • Nov 7–Dec 6 (approx.) Festival Xinshe Sea of Flowers: Vast flower fields in Xinshe, Taichung: Taiwan's biggest autumn-winter flower show.
    • Nov 15–Apr 30 (approx.) Season Dahu strawberry season: Pick-your-own strawberry farms in Dahu, Miaoli, open from November to April. Quality peaks from mid-December to Lunar New Year; from February there is more fruit and prices drop. Sources: travel.yam.com, bella.tw

  10. December

    • Dec 24–25 Public holiday Constitution Day: Restored as a public holiday from 2025; falls on Christmas Day.
    • Dec 31 Festival Taipei 101 New Year's Eve fireworks: New Year's Eve countdown fireworks on Taipei 101; roads around Xinyi are closed.

World Heritage sites

Taiwan is not a UN member and cannot nominate World Heritage sites; its Ministry of Culture lists 18 "potential sites", including Taroko, the Alishan Forest Railway, Yushan and Kinmen's battlefield culture.
